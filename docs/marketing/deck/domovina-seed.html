Insight

Nobody sells a moment.

Contextual tools

They stop at the episode

Sounder (Triton, 2024) and Barometer (The Trade Desk) score a whole episode for brand suitability from its transcript. Neither picks a moment inside it.

Ad-break cutters

They hunt silence, not subject

Every tool on the market does silence detection and nothing else. It finds a pause, never a change of topic.

The intersection

Empty

Our research turned up no product that picks the exact semantic moment. That capability does not exist.

We are already sitting on what it takes: diarised transcripts with topics and time anchors across 3,047 hours.

Mechanism

The price climbs by rule, and being outbid is good news.

You buy at a price. From that moment on, the price at which someone can take your place is public — always 50% above what you paid. You get 72 hours to match it. For the first 30 days nobody can touch you.

The outgoing brand
€450

Paid €400. Full refund plus a share of the uplift — and it ran for 45 days.

Creator, all in
€520

The whole primary price, then 60% of the uplift. For the same ten minutes.

The incoming brand
€600

Gets the moment. Its price becomes the next rung on the ladder.

Platform
€30

15% of the uplift. The only money the platform sees on this transaction.

An advertiser who loses the slot has not been burned — it advertised for free and made money doing it.

Figures are illustrative arithmetic, not a rate card. A Harberger tax was considered and rejected: griefing, self-assessment on a thin market, a standing tax, and permanent unease for the brand.

Why now

Three things line up for the first time.

Regulation

The DSA is killing person-level targeting

Article 26 requires disclosure of who paid; 26(3) bans profiling on special categories with no consent exemption. Context is the only thing left standing — and we target the content, not the person.

Technology

Transcription became cheap

Five years ago, segmenting a full episode semantically would have cost more than the ad on it is worth. Not any more.

Market habit

Buying old content is already normal

Dynamic ad insertion accounts for over 90% of podcast advertising revenue. Brands are already used to buying inside a back catalogue.

07 / 14

Model

Zero percent on the primary sale is customer acquisition, not a model.

Why 0%

The cost of acquiring creators, paid by giving up commission instead of by a marketing budget. It is the reason a creator turns up at all.

Revenue today

15% of the uplift on every buyout. Optionality, not a plan — secondary liquidity does not arrive on its own.

Revenue at scale

Yield on the reserve. In a closed euro loop the money never leaves, and the yield stays with the issuer.

Float arithmetic
€45M → €1M

An average float of ~€45M at the ECB deposit rate of 2.25% earns ~€1M gross a year. MiCA Article 50 forbids paying interest to the holder, but the issuer may keep the yield on the reserve — the model Circle and Monerium run on.

The limit we raise ourselves
~0 today

At today's scale the float is negligible, and issuing our own token requires a licence. In this deck that is a path, not revenue.

Flywheel

A brand's search query is our outreach list.

The corpus has two layers: what we can sell, and what we cannot sell yet — and the second layer turns demand into supply.

Layer B  ·  buyable

Channels inside our pipeline

Fully processed, with slot maps and prices. The search result carries a "buy this moment" button.

Layer V  ·  visible

The world's podcasts, straight from RSS

Transcript and vectors only. The result carries "tell the creator a brand is looking for them" — and that creator claims the channel, which moves it into Layer B.

Demand pulls supply through. A competitor structurally cannot run this, because it has nothing to sell.

Podscan.fm transcribes 4M+ podcasts within ~10 minutes of publication and sells brand alerts from $100/month. Search is not the moat — being able to transact is.

11 / 14

Risks

The holes we found before you did.

  • No revenue. Not a single sponsored moment has been paid for yet.
  • The pilot channel has 28 followers. It proves the mechanism, not demand. The first sponsors will be local businesses.
  • There is no public CPM data for Croatian podcasts. The first rate card is a guess.
  • Creator payout is unfinished for third-party channels — the on-chain leg is still stubbed.
  • Five legal questions are waiting on counsel — MiCA, sponsorship disclosure, VAT on the three-party buyout flow.
  • Patent freedom-to-operate is not done. The Nasdaq/NYIAX patents cover an order book, and our mechanism is bilateral and therefore outside them — but three of the four have not been checked for European family members.
  • Nobody has measured tolerance for a sponsor mark. YouTube shut its own overlay format down in 2023.
  • Secondary liquidity may never show up. NYIAX spent six years trying with Nasdaq's technology and failed — which is exactly why the model does not depend on it.
